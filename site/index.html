what this is

At work, tasks live in a system called Jira. Things happen to my tasks all day: someone comments, someone asks me a question, a new task lands on my plate. Jira's own way of telling you is email, and it buries the news.

Jira-Alerts is a small program I wrote that checks my tickets every five minutes and sends me a direct message in Microsoft Teams, the chat app my team already lives in. One short line, at the right moment, then it stays out of the way.

why I built it

I wanted to know what tickets were assigned to me and to be the one on top of them. My first try was a Jira folder in Outlook, since Jira already emails you about everything. That didn't work. I had to remember to navigate to the folder, the formatting of Jira's emails is horrible, and it piled up with things from last week when all I really needed was what's relevant on my tickets now. So why not put it in our main communication instead? Teams stays on at all times, on every desktop I use and on my Mac. What better place for Jira news to live than the main source of communication on our team.

01someone comments

A teammate comments on one of my tickets. I get their name and what they said. My own comments never ping me: my words are noise, not news.

02someone mentions me

If anyone tags me by name, on any ticket anywhere, I hear about it.

it knows when to stay quiet

  • Turning it on for the first time does not replay history. It quietly took in 42 existing tasks without a single ping.
  • If a burst of updates arrives at once, it sends one summary message instead of a pile of notifications.

honest notes

  • It runs on my own computer, so news arrives about five minutes after it happens, and not while the laptop sleeps. The next check catches up.
  • It is a personal tool and if you want your own, the repo walks anyone through it in about 30 minutes.
